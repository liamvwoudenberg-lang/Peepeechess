# Computer opponent (`godot/chess/ai/`)

A computer opponent for Classic Chess and Battle Chess C0, written in GDScript on top of the repository's own rules engine.
**Everything below is software-only and measured on a shared Linux CI-class machine under the headless Godot 4.6.3; no phone,
no GPU and no human has played it.** Strength statements are relative (level 6 vs level 1 in self-play), not Elo claims.

## Files

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `chess_ai.gd` (`ChessAI`) | Facade: six levels, engine cloning, seeds, time/node scaling, `choose_move(engine)` (synchronous), `combat_profile(level)`. |
| `chess_search.gd` (`ChessSearch`) | Iterative-deepening alpha-beta (PVS), transposition table, quiescence, ordering, pruning, Battle chance nodes. Resumable (`begin` / `step`). |
| `chess_eval.gd` (`ChessEval`) | Tapered material + piece-square tables (generated by formulas), pawn structure, bishop pair, rook files, king shelter, mop-up. |
| `chess_zobrist.gd` (`ChessZobrist`) | Fixed-seed Zobrist hashing (identical on every machine). |
| `battle_model.gd` (`ChessBattleModel`) | Duel win probabilities and fight values for Battle Chess. |
| `ai_job.gd` (`ChessAIJob`) | Runs one search on a worker `Thread` (or frame-sliced), with `cancel()`. |
| `computer_opponent.gd` (`ComputerOpponent`) | Node that plays one colour of a `ClassicMatch` / `BattleChessMatch`: delay, clock, undo, resign, draws, Battle seat. |

UI and glue outside this folder: `ui/opponent_ui.gd` (selector widgets), `app/main_opponent.gd` (game-screen glue),
`boards/board_interaction.gd` (`allowed_side`), `app/battle_combat_adapter.gd` (`computer_seat`).

## Rules are never re-implemented

The search uses `ClassicChess` / `BattleChessRules` as they are: `pseudo_moves()` for generation, `_apply()` / `_restore()` to make and undo
moves, `in_check()` after the move for legality (exactly how `legal_moves()` filters), `attacked()`, `insufficient_material()`. The root move
list is the engine's own `legal_moves()` (for Battle Chess that already contains the capture restrictions); inside the tree Battle captures
are filtered with `capture_restriction()`. A search runs on a private clone of the position, never on the live match. The computer plays
through `game.play()` like a human UI, so the match's own admission, clock and Battle-flow checks still apply. Tests verify every chosen
move against `legal_moves()` in 42 self-play games and in Battle games played through `BattleChessMatch`.

## Levels

Limits are per move. A search stops at the first of: depth cap, node cap, time budget, cancellation. `Noise` perturbs root scores,
`Margin` is the window of moves that may be picked, `Blunder` the chance to pick a random move inside that window instead of the best.

| Level | Name | Depth cap | Quiescence | Nodes | Time | Noise | Margin | Blunder | Duel skill | Defending / attacking style |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | Beginner | 1 | 0 | 400 | 0.3 s | 90 | 330 | 30 % | 0.20 | balanced / balanced |
| 2 | Casual | 2 | 2 | 1 500 | 0.6 s | 55 | 220 | 12 % | 0.38 | balanced / balanced |
| 3 | Club | 3 | 4 | 4 000 | 1.0 s | 25 | 110 | 4 % | 0.55 | balanced / balanced |
| 4 | Advanced | 5 | 6 | 9 000 | 1.5 s | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.70 | defensive / aggressive |
| 5 | Expert | 7 | 8 | 18 000 | 2.5 s | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.82 | duelist / aggressive |
| 6 | Strong | 12 | 10 | 40 000 | 4.0 s | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.92 | duelist / aggressive |

Check extensions can search a few plies deeper than the cap along forcing lines. Under-promotion: only a knight is ever considered besides the
queen, and it is chosen only if its score beats the queen's by more than 60 cp (mate, stalemate avoidance, a fork).

### Speed (software-only measurement)

About 5 000 nodes/s in tactical middlegames and 9 000-13 000 nodes/s from the opening position, in GDScript with the repository engine's
dictionary-based move generator (a node costs a `pseudo_moves()` call, ~0.15-0.25 ms). On that machine, level 6 reaches depth 5-6 in its
4 s budget. Mobile CPUs will be slower: `ChessAI.time_scale` shrinks every budget (the controller uses 0.6 when the `mobile` feature is
present), and the node caps bound the work regardless of speed. This is a scale estimate, not a device benchmark.

## Determinism

`ChessAI.new(level, seed)` seeds all randomness (noise, blunders) and Zobrist keys are fixed, so for a given level, seed and sequence of
positions the chosen moves are identical **when the search ends on depth / node limits** (`ai.deterministic = true` removes the wall clock;
tests use it). With a wall-clock limit the depth reached depends on machine speed, so moves can differ between machines.

## Not blocking the UI, cancellation

`ChessAIJob` runs the same search on a worker `Thread`; the owner polls once per frame. On a single-threaded web export (no `threads` feature)
it falls back to frame-sliced search: each frame `step(6 ms)` runs, and an interrupted depth resumes through the transposition table.
`cancel()` sets a flag checked every 64 nodes and joins the thread (measured well under a second in tests). `ComputerOpponent` cancels on
new game, undo, resign, pause, game end, a changed position and when it leaves the tree.

## Battle Chess heuristic

In Battle Chess a capture starts a duel whose winner is decided by combat. The search therefore treats every capture as a **chance node**:

`value = p * (attacker wins: ordinary capture, searched) + (1 - p) * (defender wins: attacker removed, defender stays, turn passes, evaluated statically)`

`p` is the attacking class's win probability against the defending class from the combat owner's bot matrix
(`combat/data/balance_matrix_v1.json`, the `balanced` style at skill 0.6, N = 8 fights per ordered pair): `(wins + 1) / (N + 2)`, clamped to
[0.08, 0.92]. Examples: Pawn takes Queen 0.10, Queen takes Pawn 0.90, Knight takes Pawn 0.90, Pawn takes Pawn 0.60. This is **bot-measured**, based on 8 fights
per pair, and says nothing about human play; if the file is missing a class-tier fallback is used. The defender-wins outcome is built with
`BattleChessRules._apply_defender_win()`, the same transition the match applies. Material uses duel strength (Pawn 90, Knight 340, Bishop 300, Rook 430,
Queen 900) instead of orthodox values. Quiescence is disabled in Battle Chess (captures are gambles, not exchanges); kings never start duels
(`capture_restriction`), mate and stalemate use Battle-legal moves, and a position with no Battle-legal move returns no move. In the duel
itself the computer's colour is a `CombatAI` seat (`computer_seat` on `BattleCombatAdapter`): skill from the table above, and a *reactive*
style (defensive / duelist) only when defending. Reactive styles never start a fight and a tie goes to the defender, so an attacking
computer uses `aggressive` (verified against an idle human in `tests/ai/ai_combat_test.gd`).

## Computer opponent behaviour

- Plays after a seeded 0.5-0.9 s delay, shortened when animations are fast or off, and waits for `animation_busy` (board animation).
- Clock: about 1/30 of the remaining time (+ half the increment), never more than the level budget; under 5 s it plays in <= 120 ms.
- Draw offers: accepts when its last score is <= -90 cp, or in a long (>= 80 plies) level game; otherwise declines. It claims a Classic
  threefold / fifty-move draw when not better than +20 cp. It resigns (Classic, level >= 3) after three consecutive moves at <= -1100 cp past
  move 30. Battle Chess draws: only the human's offer is answered; the computer never resigns there.
- Undo (Classic): two plies on the human's turn, one while the computer is still thinking. Battle Chess has no undo (rules).
- The human can only select their own colour (`BoardInteraction.allowed_side`); the board is flipped when the human plays Black.

## Limits

No opening book, no endgame tablebases, no pondering, no multi-threaded search, no learning. The evaluation is generic and untuned beyond
the self-play checks in `tests/ai`. In Battle Chess the AI cannot see duel skill (it assumes the matrix probabilities), the defender-wins branch
is static, and its fight model is not a prediction of how a particular human fights. Level 6 is "strong for a hobby engine in an interpreted
language", not a tournament engine.

## Tests

`npm run test:ai` (`scripts/test-ai.mjs`; suites in `godot/tests/ai/`): `ai_rules_test` (42 seeded self-play games at levels 1-6, legality, rules
results, determinism), `ai_tactics_test` (9 mate-in-1, 8 mate-in-2, 6 mate-in-3, 8 material tactics, time budget, worker thread, cancellation,
sliced mode), `ai_strength_test` (level 6 vs 1, 10 games, colours alternate), `ai_battle_test` (full Battle games through `BattleChessMatch` with
auto-resolved duels, capture heuristic), `ai_combat_test` (real arena, AI seats), `ai_ui_test` (opponent selection, game screen, undo, draw,
resign, board settings apply + persist).
