# World assets (chess set, board dressing, pit set)

Generated by `tools/blender/` (Blender 4.3.0 as the `bpy` module, numpy 1.26.4; see `tools/blender/README.md` for the toolchain).
Nothing here is hand-edited and nothing is downloaded: every mesh and texture comes from Python, every texture has a fixed seed.
Source of truth for Godot is the committed GLBs, PNGs, `.tres` materials and their `.import` files. Owner: Claude Code
(`docs/development/OWNERSHIP.md`, the `godot/assets/world/**` path was added with this work). The world presentation worker consumes it.

Regenerate (about 6 s):

```sh
tools/blender/setup_env.sh .venv-blender                       # once: pinned bpy + numpy
.venv-blender/bin/python tools/blender/build_all.py --only world        # pieces + board + pit
.venv-blender/bin/python tools/blender/build_all.py --only pieces       # or board / pit alone
.venv-blender/bin/python tools/blender/build_all.py --only world --out /tmp/scratch   # scratch build under /tmp/scratch/world
godot --headless --path godot --editor --import                          # after regenerating: refresh Godot's import cache
godot --headless --path godot --script res://combat/tests/test_runner.gd -- world_assets
```

The GLB bytes are not bit-identical between two runs (the glTF exporter orders the indices of identical triangles differently);
vertices, node names, materials, textures and animation data are identical.

## Conventions

* Metres. Godot space: +Y up, the board tile is 1.0 m, tile boxes are 0.995 x 0.08 x 0.995 centred on y = 0 (tile top y = +0.04).
  Pieces face Godot **+Z** (rotate the node 180 deg for the other side); pivot = base centre on y = 0.
* Material names are stable and meaningful (`Piece_Light_Gold`, `Tile_Dark`, `Pit_Wood`, ...). Textures are embedded in the GLBs; the same
  PNGs are also stored in `textures/` (inputs to the GLBs, max 512 px) so Godot-side `StandardMaterial3D`s can share them.
* Visual only: no colliders, no scripts. Empties are plain `Node3D` (Marker3D-style); swap them for `Marker3D` in code if wanted.

## Inventory (tris are triangles of the whole GLB)

### `pieces/` - six chess pieces x light/dark x LOD0/LOD1

`<role>_<side>.glb` and `<role>_<side>_lod1.glb`, role in pawn knight bishop rook queen king, side in light dark. 12 meshes (the
geometry is identical per role; only the materials differ: light = royal ivory marble + gold trim + sapphire studs, dark = skeleton
basalt + bone trim + crimson studs). Root node `Piece_<Role>_<Side>` (empty at the base centre), mesh child `Piece_<Role>_<Side>_Mesh`;
three or four surfaces: body (textured marble / basalt, 256 px), trim (metal), detail (emissive-tinted studs / gems), recess (eye,
slit, arrow slits).

| Role | base dia | height | LOD0 tris | LOD1 tris | plan silhouette from above |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| pawn | 0.62 | 0.70 | 1344 | 624 | concentric rings: collar, brim, dome |
| knight | 0.68 | 1.00 | 1004 | 416 | elongated head + mane ridge pointing forward (+Z) |
| bishop | 0.68 | 1.04 | 1224 | 576 | squashed ellipse with a diagonal dark slit |
| rook | 0.70 | 0.85 | 804 | 444 | octagon with 8 corner merlons |
| queen | 0.72 | 1.19 | 1620 | 648 | sunburst of 8 crown spikes around a jewel |
| king | 0.72 | 1.31 | 1420 | 672 | "+" cross over four fleurons |

Every base has a trim ring and 12 studs (LOD0) so it reads from above. Budget: <= 2500 tris per piece (max is the queen, 1620).
LOD1 uses 12 radial segments and drops studs / ears / gems: swap it by distance or on mobile.

### `board/` - dressing for Codex's board (Codex's tile size and positions are NOT changed)

* `tile_light.glb`, `tile_dark.glb` - one tile (0.995 x 0.08 x 0.995, beveled 6 mm) with the marble top texture (UV 1:1 on the top face).
  `textures/Tile_Light.png`, `Tile_Dark.png` are 512 px, seamless (checked by the test), `materials/tile_light.tres`, `tile_dark.tres` are
  ready `StandardMaterial3D`s using them: `board_3d.gd` can assign them to its box tiles instead of flat colours.
* `board_frame.glb` - root `BoardFrame`: `BoardFrame_Mesh` (square ring, inner edge at |x| = |z| = 4.0, plaque field 4.14..4.64, gold-inlay
  channel + outer bead, top y = 0.13, plinth skirt down to y = -0.60 with an overhang), `BoardBase_Mesh` (slab under the tiles, grout colour,
  top y = 0, shows through the 5 mm tile gaps), `CornerPost_{NE,NW,SE,SW}_Mesh`. 780 tris. It can replace Codex's plinth + gold border boxes.
* `board_plaques.glb` - root `BoardPlaques`, 32 nodes: `plaque_file_<a-h>_<s|n>` at (x = i - 3.5, z = +-4.39) and
  `plaque_rank_<1-8>_<w|e>` at (x = +-4.39, z = r - 4.5), i.e. exactly `Interaction.world_position()` (rank 8 is at +Z). Each node has a
  `_slab` and a `_glyph` child (raised gold letter). `_s` and the rank plaques read upright for a camera on +Z; `_n` is turned 180 deg
  for a camera on -Z. To flip the board view, rotate the plaque NODES about Y (their origin is the plaque centre). 4032 drawn tris
  (1356 unique: the 16 glyph meshes and the slab are shared), 3 draw calls' worth of materials.
  Note for Codex: the current `Label3D` loop in `board_3d.gd` puts the label "8" at z = -3.5 while `world_position()` puts rank 8 at z = +3.5.

### `pit/`

* `trapdoor_light.glb`, `trapdoor_dark.glb` - ONE tile (0.995 x 0.995 x 0.08) made of two leaves; the top is the tile material of the matching
  tile colour (marble continues across the seam), the underside is wood planks + iron straps + hinge barrels (584 tris).
  Nodes: root `Trapdoor_<Side>`; `hinge_left` (Node3D pivot at x = -0.4975, y = 0, z = 0) containing mesh `leaf_left`;
  `hinge_right` (x = +0.4975) containing `leaf_right`. **Open = rotate `hinge_left` by -90 deg and `hinge_right` by +90 deg about Z**
  (rotation.z in radians: -PI/2 and +PI/2); the free edges then hang down the shaft wall; use about +-100 deg for a slam/overshoot.
  Verified by `test_world_assets.gd` (open leaves hang down).
* `shaft_rim.glb` - root `ShaftRim`, square liner: opening 1.0 x 1.0, outer 1.4 (lip 1.48), 0.5 m tall, top at y = -0.04 (flush under the
  tile layer), course joints every 0.25 m, 88 tris. Place at the tile centre.
* `shaft_wall_segment.glb` - root `ShaftWallSegment`, 1.0 m tall square tube (inner 1.0, outer 1.4), origin at the bottom centre, V-grooves
  every 0.25 m with half grooves at both ends so stacking is seamless (the stone texture is 2.0 m per tile = 8 courses of 0.25 m). 120 tris.
  The rim bottom is at y = -0.54, so segments sit at y = -1.54, -2.54, ... (bottom of each).
* Ladder (`ladder_segment_2m.glb`, `ladder_top.glb`, `ladder_full_10m.glb`) - ladder plane at z = 0 (rung centres), rails at x = +-0.28
  (0.62 m wide), climbing direction +Y, rungs every 0.25 m at y = (j + 0.5) * 0.25. Wood rails/rungs, iron collars. 
  * segment (root `LadderSegment2m`): y in [0, 2], 8 rungs, anchors `00..07`; 552 tris. Stack by adding 2.0 m (rungs stay on the grid).
  * top (root `LadderTop`): y in [-1.0, +0.75], rails continue 0.75 m above the rim for hand-holds, 7 rungs (anchors `00..06`), iron
    hooks that lie over the shaft edge (arms at y = +0.046, z up to +0.35 behind the rails, cross bar at z = 0.35); 560 tris. Origin = the
    tile mid-plane; the hook arms rest on the tile top (y = 0.04).
  * full (root `LadderFull10m`): top section + 4 segments, y in [-9.0, +0.75] (9.75 m), 39 rungs, anchors `000..038` from the bottom; 2416 tris.
  * Anchors (Node3D at each rung, L = +X = the climber's left): `rung_NN` (rung centre, x = 0), `hand_L_NN` / `hand_R_NN` (x = +-0.17, rung
    centre), `foot_L_NN` / `foot_R_NN` (x = +-0.15, rung top = centre + 0.022).
  * Climber placement (matches the `climb` clip): the character origin is at **z = -0.42** in the ladder frame (it faces +Z, towards the
    ladder), and at clip phase 0 and 0.5 its origin is at y = n * 0.25 (halfway between two rungs), then it rises at
    `CLIMB_SPEED_MPS = 0.4167` m/s while `climb` plays at speed 1.0 (72 frames = 1.2 s = two rungs). In the shaft: with the shaft centre
    at the tile centre, put the ladder at the shaft centre + (0, 0, +0.42): the rails (z +-0.035) then sit 0.045 from the wall (z = 0.5).
* `chamber_ceiling.glb` - root `ChamberCeiling`: 16 x 16 m dark-stone slab, top at y = 0, 0.6 thick, hole 1.0 x 1.0 at the centre with a 0.15 m
  collar (1.4 outer), underside coffer beams to y = -0.94; 320 tris. Square (not round) so that the hole is exact; it covers the 15.4 m arena wall ring.
* `light_cone.glb` - root `LightCone`: open square frustum, unit height (y in [-1, 0]), 1.0 x 1.0 at the top, 2.8 x 2.8 at the bottom, vertex alpha
  0.55 -> 0 (8 tris). Scale Y to the shaft depth (a Y scale of 10 widens the beam by only 0.9 m per side). Use
  `materials/light_cone.tres` (unshaded, additive, vertex colours, double sided) as `material_override`: glTF cannot express additive blending.

## Using it in Godot

```gdscript
var piece: Node3D = load("res://assets/world/pieces/knight_light.glb").instantiate()   # base centre on y = 0 (tile top is y = 0.04)
piece.position = Vector3(world_x, 0.04, world_z)
var door: Node3D = load("res://assets/world/pit/trapdoor_dark.glb").instantiate()
tween.tween_property(door.find_child("hinge_left"), "rotation:z", -PI / 2.0, 0.35)
tween.parallel().tween_property(door.find_child("hinge_right"), "rotation:z", PI / 2.0, 0.35)
```

Fighter clips for the sequence (`godot/assets/combat/characters/<class>/<class>.glb`, also in `_lod1`): `drop_fall` (loop, 40 f),
`land` (one-shot, 36 f = 0.6 s), `climb` (loop, 72 f), `ladder_idle` (loop, 120 f). `CharacterModel.LOOPING` includes the three loops. The clips
do not move the root: move the character yourself (fall with gravity, climb at 0.4167 m/s). While climbing the weapon is slung back
and outward and a shield faces sideways, but they were authored for the unscaled 1.8 m fighters: you may want to hide props or scale
the character down to fit a 1.0 m opening (the Rook's shield and the King/Knight bulk are wider than the 0.62 m ladder).

ladder segment 552, top 560, full 2416; ceiling 320; cone 8. A full start position (32 pieces) is about 40k tris at LOD0 (about 18k at LOD1).

## Known issues

* The climb knees reach about 0.40-0.48 m in front of the origin, i.e. they brush the rung plane at 0.42 m (a few cm of knee/rung overlap
  while a knee is raised); the feet slide up to 2 cm laterally during a hold (pelvis yaw); hands and feet are on the grid to within 0.1 mm.
* Weapons and shields were not re-modelled for climbing; the Rook's shield and big pauldrons overlap the ladder rails.
* Pieces are low-poly stylised lathe shapes: the knight is the least refined; no normal maps, no baked AO.
* Dark pieces on dark tiles rely on the bone trim ring for contrast; if the final lighting is dim, raise `Piece_Dark_Basalt`'s albedo.
* Tile/stone textures are procedural noise with veins/blocks, not photo-real; the plaque letters use Blender's built-in font.
* The ceiling is square; if the chamber needs a round cap, intersect it with a cylinder in Godot or ask for a variant.
